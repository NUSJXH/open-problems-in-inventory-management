Editorial manual v1.0

Inventory must be the mechanism, not merely the setting.

The registry uses a two-gate boundary test, three evidence types, separate research-status and workflow codes, and a dated literature audit. This keeps a promising agenda from being mislabeled as an unsolved theorem.

Boundary

The two-gate test

01

Operations at the heart

Is the central question about designing, planning, controlling, or improving an operating process?

→
02

Inventory at the heart

Do stock-flow state, inventory actions, and intertemporal inventory trade-offs determine the result?

→
03

Deletion test

If inventory balance and availability are removed, does the problem disappear or materially change?

Evidence taxonomy

How a record enters the registry

Type A

Explicit formal problem

The source states a precise open, unknown, unresolved, complexity, policy-structure, or bound question.

Still requires a current-status audit.
Type B

Explicit research challenge

The source identifies an important structural, computational, empirical, or implementation challenge.

Must not be presented as a theorem or conjecture.
Type C

Editorially synthesized candidate

Editors combine multiple sources into a sharper candidate question and disclose that synthesis.

Candidate only; source authors did not state the exact question.

Verification

A record earns trust in stages

  1. EX
    Extracted

    Candidate text and source pointer recorded.

  2. SV
    Source verified

    A human checks the exact passage, location, metadata, and scope.

  3. LA
    Literature audited

    Backward, forward, semantic, and author/version searches are logged.

  4. EV
    Expert verified

    A conflict-free domain expert confirms the statement and dated current status.

Important: EX, SV, LA, and EV should be stored as separate completed steps. A composite label such as “SV-LA (provisional)” is not sufficiently auditable.
